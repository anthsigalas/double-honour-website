Frequently asked questions

Questions churches ask us

Funding structures and property purchases vary by church. These answers explain the general approach; where terms depend on your circumstances, we will say so rather than imply a standard answer.

Funding

Can our church get a mortgage?

Many churches use a combination of their own savings and traditional bank finance. Double Honour can work with churches where conventional lending alone is not sufficient, subject to the church’s circumstances and the proposed property purchase.

How much can we borrow?

There is no single borrowing amount that applies to every church. We begin by understanding your financial position, property requirements, fundraising progress and existing funding options before structuring an appropriate solution.

How much deposit do we need?

Deposit requirements depend on the property, the senior lender and the overall funding structure. Double Honour’s role can include helping bridge a funding gap where church savings and traditional lending do not cover the full purchase price.

Can Double Honour help if we don't have enough deposit?

Potentially, yes. Bridging the gap between church savings, traditional lending and the purchase price is one of the core situations Double Honour is designed to address.

What is a church loan?

In this context, it is funding provided to support a church or eligible church-related charity with a property purchase. The exact structure will depend on the transaction and the organisation involved.

What interest rates do you charge?

Double Honour’s current website materials do not set out a standard public rate. Funding terms are structured for the individual transaction and will be explained before any commitment is made.

How long is the loan term?

Loan terms are considered case by case. Please contact Double Honour with your circumstances so the team can discuss an appropriate structure.

Are there any fees?

Any applicable fees and costs depend on the structure of the transaction. Double Honour will set out the relevant terms as part of the funding process.

Eligibility

Which churches do you work with?

Double Honour supports churches and church networks seeking property solutions for ministry. Each enquiry is assessed on its own circumstances, structure and property needs.

Can charities borrow from Double Honour?

Double Honour works with churches and church-related charitable structures. The appropriate borrower and ownership structure is considered as part of the transaction.

Can a small church apply?

Yes, a smaller church can still start a conversation with Double Honour. Suitability depends on the organisation’s financial position, property requirement and proposed funding plan rather than size alone.

Do you only help evangelical churches?

Double Honour describes itself as a Christian organisation serving churches. If you are unsure whether your church fits Double Honour’s remit, please contact the team and they can confirm.

Can newly planted churches apply?

Newly planted churches can contact Double Honour. The team will look at the church’s financial position, fundraising progress, governance and property requirements as part of the assessment.

Can CIOs and charitable trusts apply?

The legal and ownership structure needs to be considered for each transaction. Please contact Double Honour with your organisation details so the team can advise on fit.

Do we need audited accounts?

The current website materials do not prescribe a single document standard for every applicant. Double Honour will confirm what financial information is needed after the initial discussion.

Buying a Manse

Can you help us buy a manse?

Yes. Helping churches purchase residential property for ministry is at the centre of Double Honour’s work, from funding strategy through property search, negotiation and completion support.

Can the minister live in the property?

Double Honour’s model is designed around housing for ministers and other gospel workers, subject to the legal and transaction structure of the particular purchase.

Can we buy a house before selling our current property?

This depends on the timing, existing property, available capital and lending structure. Double Honour can review the position with you and assess whether a workable funding plan is possible.

What types of properties can be funded?

Double Honour’s current materials focus primarily on residential property for ministry. Suitability is assessed in the context of the church’s needs and the proposed purchase.

Can we buy at auction?

Auction purchases have tighter deadlines and require funding certainty. Double Honour’s current materials do not set out a standard auction policy, so please discuss the property and timetable with the team before bidding.

Application Process

How does the application process work?

Double Honour starts with a discovery consultation, then helps the church prepare to buy, find the right property, negotiate and secure it, manage the purchase and complete the handover.

What documents do we need?

The exact documents depend on the church and transaction. As a starting point, Double Honour will need enough information to understand your financial position, fundraising progress, governance and property requirements.

How long does approval take?

Timing depends on the complexity of the church, the proposed property and the funding structure. Double Honour will be able to give a clearer timetable after the initial consultation.

What happens after we apply?

Double Honour reviews your ministry needs, property requirements and financial position, then works with you on a tailored property and funding strategy and the steps required to become ready to buy.

When can funds be released?

Funding can only be released once the agreed transaction requirements and documentation are in place. The exact timing is specific to each purchase.

Will you work with our solicitor and mortgage lender?

Yes. Double Honour’s process includes coordinating with professionals such as solicitors, surveyors, valuers, mortgage advisers and lenders to help the transaction progress.

Funding Structure

What if the bank won't lend enough?

That is one of the main funding gaps Double Honour is designed to address. Double Honour can assess whether additional flexible capital can sit alongside the church’s savings and traditional lending.

Can Double Honour work alongside another lender?

Yes. Double Honour’s model can work alongside senior or traditional lenders as part of the overall capital structure, subject to the specific transaction.

Do you provide second-charge lending?

The exact security and ranking of Double Honour funding depends on the transaction. Please contact the team for current terms rather than relying on a standard structure.

Can Double Honour be our main lender?

That will depend on the church, the property and the funding requirement. Double Honour will assess the most appropriate capital structure as part of the initial work.

What if we don't qualify for a traditional mortgage?

Double Honour can still review the position with you. The team’s role includes looking beyond a standard mortgage and assessing whether a different sustainable funding structure is possible.

Consultancy & Support

Do you only provide funding?

No. Double Honour also supports churches with financial analysis, funding structure, fundraising advice, property identification, negotiation and transaction support through to completion.

Can you recommend solicitors, surveyors and financial advisers?

Double Honour’s process includes coordinating with trusted professionals such as solicitors, surveyors, valuers, mortgage advisers and lenders. The most appropriate advisers will depend on the transaction.

Will you help us throughout the purchase process?

Yes. Double Honour’s current process is designed as end-to-end support, from the first discovery consultation through to completion and handover.

Can you review our finances before we make an offer?

Yes. Understanding the church’s financial position and structuring a sustainable funding solution are core parts of Double Honour’s support before a purchase progresses.

Do you help negotiate with lenders?

Double Honour supports capital structuring and works alongside other lenders where appropriate. The team can help coordinate the funding process and negotiate transaction terms.

Costs & Repayments

How are repayments calculated?

Repayment terms depend on the funding structure agreed for the transaction. Double Honour will explain the economics and repayment profile before the church commits.

Can we repay early?

Early repayment terms are transaction-specific. Please ask Double Honour to confirm the flexibility available under the proposed funding structure.

Are there early repayment charges?

The current materials do not publish a standard policy. Any early repayment provisions will be set out clearly in the agreed terms.

Are there any hidden fees?

Double Honour will set out the applicable funding terms and transaction costs before commitment. If a particular cost is relevant to your structure, it should be addressed during the process.

What happens if our circumstances change?

Double Honour describes its funding approach as flexible and tailored. If circumstances change, speak to the team as early as possible so the position can be reviewed.

About Double Honour

Why choose Double Honour instead of a bank?

Double Honour is designed to complement situations where traditional lending alone does not solve the property funding need. It combines flexible funding with specialist church, property and finance support.

Is Double Honour a Christian organisation?

Yes. Double Honour describes itself as a Christian company helping churches buy manses for ministry and as a long-term partner seeking to serve churches with integrity and stewardship.

How does Double Honour support churches beyond lending?

Double Honour supports churches with financial analysis, capital structuring, fundraising advice, property search and assessment, negotiation and support through to completion.

What experience does Double Honour have?

The current materials describe more than 160 years of combined team experience across residential property, valuation, investor advisory, private equity, residential development, financial products, law and investment management.
